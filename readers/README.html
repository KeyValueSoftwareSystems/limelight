The product is the file, not the light show

We listen to a song once and write down what it does. That file is the map.

A reader then turns the map into an art form. Lighting is the first reader. A drone show is the second. Neither one is the product, and that distinction is the whole bet: if the map is right, every future reader is cheap. If the map is wrong, every reader inherits the same blindness.

So the interesting work is not the show. It is deciding what a song is, in fields.

How it fits together

Three tiers, three different promises

Everything measured goes in the file. What changes between tiers is not the data — it is what we promise about it. That was the insight that unlocked this: the minimalist rule was never about what to measure, it was about what to promise.

A field goes in when a reader breaks without it.

Two fields earned their place that way, and both were found by a reader failing, not by planning. stems went in because the lighting reader wanted to spotlight the voice and had no way to find the voice. accents went in because a reader wanted to stab three times at second two. 71% of the drum hits in this song miss the beat grid — though only 5% miss the sixteenth, which is the real shape of the programming — so neither beats nor moments could supply those times.

Be miserly with promises. Be generous with observations.

Five words, and who owns each

The whole system is five nouns. If you find yourself needing a sixth, say it out loud — it usually means something is designed wrong.

Map and layout are nouns. The frame is a verb. The map says what the music is doing, the layout says what is in the room, and the frame answers “so what should be on right now”.

Giving feedback: say the bar, not the millisecond.

Bar N starts at 0.760 + (N−1) × 1.905 seconds. Bar 40 is about 1:15. “The build at bar 40 feels late” is enough for me to fix it — the tools snap to the nearest beat anyway, so chasing the exact millisecond wastes your time.

One rule makes a reader testable

frame = f(map, layout, recipe, t)

Ask for t = 47 twice and get identical bytes. Ask for t = 47 without having played the first 47 seconds and still get the right answer. That is what lets us scrub during rehearsal, join a show late, and test against golden frames at all.

It costs something real, and this is the part worth arguing about: an effect cannot be a state machine. Not “on the beat, start a fade” but “brightness = f(position in the beat)”. The map is your history. “When was the last beat” is a lookup, never a memory.

Everything the map promises. spec/MAP.md is the same thing in the form the repo builds against.

learned  what nobody has a word for

369 × 768 float16, out of line because that does not belong in JSON. Pooling between beats makes it nearly tempo‑invariant for free: 126 BPM and 84 BPM give the same sequence length.

It earned its place by correcting the named tier. In the learned space the two stretches I had labelled as different drops score 0.951 — the same section. Re‑clustering on the vectors returns two identical passes of a six‑part cycle, with whole bar counts both times. That sits in the file as segmentation_proposal, recorded and not applied.

What a lighting reader outputs

This shape belongs to the lighting reader alone. It is not universal — a video editor outputs cut points, a rhythm game outputs a note chart. Normalised: nothing here knows about DMX.

FieldRangeMeaning
tsecondsWhich instant this frame is for.
lookstring Diagnostic only. Which recipe branch ran. Not part of the contract, but the first thing to compare when a frame is wrong.
r g b0–255 Colour. When level is 0 these are 0 too — off means actually zero.
level0–1 Brightness, linear. Display gamma is the room’s business, not the frame’s.
pan tilt0–1 Where a moving light points, as a fraction of its own range.
strobeHz 0 means steady. Capped by the layout’s max_strobe_hz, never by the recipe.
pixels[[r,g,b], …] Strips only. Brightness is baked into the rgb; strips have no level.

Why layout and wiring are two files

Lighting people bolt these together and call it a “patch”. They are unrelated. Layout is which fixtures exist, what each can do, and where it sits. Wiring is universe, channel, channel map and physical lag.

Splitting them buys one property worth the trouble: the browser club and the real club load the same layout file. Only the wiring differs. So what we tune in the simulator is what happens in the room by construction, not by hope — which is the entire reason the simulator can be trusted. It also settles who reads what: the browser room must never see a DMX address, and the wire code must never care about zones.

Four rules, and what is deliberately missing

  • 1
    Time is always seconds.

    Decimal, from the start of the recording. Never milliseconds, bars, samples or frames.

  • 2
    A reader ignores fields it does not recognise.

    That is what lets the file grow without a migration.

  • 3
    A field enters the interface tier when a reader breaks without it.

    Not when a fact is interesting.

  • 4
    One writer per fact.

    Two lanes computing the same number will eventually disagree, and then both are suspect.

  • absent
    No lighting words anywhere in the map.

    No hardware, channels, fixtures or colours. No tempo either, because it is already in the beats. Those live in layouts/ and in the recipe, or nowhere. The moment the map says “strobe” it becomes a lighting file and the whole idea collapses.

The universality claim, tested rather than asserted

Same map, same recipe, three rigs of different size. Fixtures are addressed by position in the room, not by index, which is the reason one recipe drives all three without a line changing.

What is wrong right now

Six things, so nobody rediscovers them. Every one of these is open on purpose rather than hidden.

  • not solved
    Drone separation.

    Craft pass 0.07 m apart against a 2.6 m minimum. Three attempts at speed limits fixed the speed and not the spacing. This needs real path planning, not another clamp.

  • disputed
    Structure from 1:03 to 1:47.

    Six independent methods disagree with my chapter boundaries, including the learned space, which scores the two “drops” at 0.951 similar — the same section. Recorded as segmentation_proposal and not applied. Structure is a human call.

  • weak
    Chords.

    Template matching agrees with the detected notes only 69.4% of the time. It is probably calling C♯ major “F♯7”. Needs a real chord model.

  • weak
    Lyric words.

    Whisper heard “What’s up on a young year” for “Once upon a younger year”. The times are good, the words are not. The fix is forced alignment: recall for the words, measurement for the times.

  • thin
    Human truth.

    One verified moment in the whole song. Everything else is measured but unconfirmed. About fifteen minutes of somebody’s ears would fix this.

  • held, not solved
    Moving‑head speed.

    Inside the rig’s declared slew limit — 0.96 against 1.05 — but held there by a blunt cap rather than by understanding. It works and it is not right.

Rules that do not bend

  • audio
    No song files in the repo. Ever.

    Scores, never songs. The mp3 ships inside the zip for local testing and stays out of git. Friday’s video uses licensed or generated audio. This is also the reason the map can be shared at all when the recording cannot.

  • truth
    Never invent a timestamp.

    A guess lives in maps/sketch/ marked "how": "sketch", never mixed with measurement. One invented timestamp makes every number after it a lie that still prints.

  • safety
    Safety lives in the layout, not the recipe.

    Strobes cap at 4 Hz. Lasers stay above 3.0 m — a hard floor, not a preference. No audience scanning. A blackout must survive the engine crashing.
