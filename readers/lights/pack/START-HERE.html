The frame problem

Everything a lighting rig does, at one instant, computed from three files and a number. Three songs, nine fixtures, one function to write.

Limelight · reader lane · v0.2 · 2 September 2026

1 · What you are building

One function. It takes the music, the room, the taste and a clock reading, and returns what every light is doing right now.

2 · How you know the music lifts from 1:05 to 1:20

This is the question worth answering before you write a line. You have no audio. You have a file. So the file must say it.

A drop is an instant, and a point in time describes it exactly. An elevation is not an instant, and there is no honest way to write it as one. So map.json carries three different shapes of fact, and each answers a different question.

Two things follow, and both are in RECIPE.md. The pulse floor and peak both climb with energy, and the decay exponent sharpens, so a build that runs for three minutes gets brighter, snappier and denser the whole way — driven entirely by one interpolated number, with no extra fields and no state.

The rule that produced this. Version 0.1 of the map had no energy curve, on the principle that you never store a derivable fact — loudness is in the audio. That was wrong, and here is the corrected version:

A fact is only redundant if the person who needs it can derive it. Energy is derivable for the analysis lane, which holds the audio, and unobtainable for you, who hold only the map. The old rule deleted information at exactly the boundary where it mattered.

3 · The room

Nine fixtures. Two moving heads are in layout.json marked "optional": true and are not in v1 frames — no pan, no tilt, no motor lag to model yet.

4 · The three songs

Chosen so that each one breaks something the previous one let you get away with.

These maps are sketches, not truth. Every one carries "how": "sketch" and "confidence": 0.3. The timings are plausible and internally consistent; they were not measured against audio. That is deliberate and it does not weaken your task one bit — your job is to reproduce frames from a map, and a sketch map is a perfectly good map for that. It does mean you must never copy these numbers into anything that claims to be truth.

6 · The four problems

Problem 1 — Read one instant

Write the map lookups: bi, beat_len, phase, bar_phase, energy, chapter, span, stop, since. Then the look selector from RECIPE.md §Look selection.

Given
a map and a t
Produce
the correct look string
Passes
every look in every keyframes file matches
Trap
Binary-search the beats array. A linear scan is 1,359 comparisons per frame on Strobe, 40 times a second — it will work and then it will not.

Problem 2 — Reproduce the keyframes

Implement the per-fixture formulas until every annotated instant matches.

Given
expected/*.keyframes.json — 36 instants across the three songs
Produce
an identical frame at each of those t values
Passes
levels within 0.01, colours within 2, strobe within 0.05 Hz
Trap
Off means actually zero — during a stop, r, g and b are 0 as well as the level. A dark red PAR is not a dark room.

Problem 3 — Reproduce every frame

Emit the full stream at 40 fps and pass the checker on all three songs. 54,280 frames.

python3 check.py expected/opus.frames.jsonl.gz mine/opus.frames.jsonl.gz
PASS  21760 frames identical within tolerance (544.0s)
Passes
three PASS lines
Trap
The return rule multiplies levels after the look is computed, and fog is exempt from it. Two ordering mistakes hide here.

Problem 4 — Prove it is pure

A property test: pick 500 random t values, render each one cold, and render the same 500 again in a different order. The frames must be byte-identical.

Passes
no divergence, in any order, with no warm-up
Trap
If you catch yourself writing "when a drop starts, begin a fade", stop. Write brightness = f(position in beat) instead. The map is your history — when was the last beat is a lookup, not a memory. This is the one constraint that buys scrubbing, joining a show late, and running on a hundred phones.

7 · What comes next, so you can see where this goes

The named fields you are reading now — spans, energy, moments — will eventually be produced by a model rather than written by hand. Some of what the model notices will have no name at all, and that residue lands in a second tier.

Which is why nothing you write now becomes disposable. Your reader compiles against names, and names are the one tier a person can correct. As the model improves, the vectors get better silently and the field values get more accurate — the shape of what you read does not move. vectors is null in all three maps today, and its slot is already specified in MAP.md so that filling it changes nothing here.

8 · If a golden frame looks wrong

Say so, with the timestamp. The golden stream is a conformance target, not a claim about music — it encodes recipe v0.2 exactly, and recipe v0.2 is a first guess at taste that will be replaced the moment something looks better. If you think a moment should look different, that is a recipe argument and a welcome one; it is settled by putting two versions of the same fifteen seconds side by side, never by rating one out of ten.

What is not negotiable is that your output matches until the recipe changes. When it changes, the golden files are regenerated and you re-run the checker.
