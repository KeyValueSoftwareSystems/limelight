## Acceptance tests — written down so nobody has to argue later

**Level one is done when:** The Nights plays end to end from a machine-written map; the blackout
lands within a twentieth of a second of where the truth file says the stop is; and **someone
outside the team watches ten seconds and asks to see it again.**

**Two gates, not one.** The **number** (maps vs truth, automatic, honest) and the **vote** (two
versions of the same fifteen seconds, which is better). Numbers cannot grade beauty; votes can.

**Curriculum check:** track the score **per level**, not just the total. If level two improves
while we were only working on level one, the method is working. That is the claim, and it is
testable.

## How we decide — three questions

1. Does the **curve** go up?
2. Did we **remove** a part or a cost?
3. Is the **moment** better?

Answer one, or it is not work. No permission needed, no meeting needed.

## Four rules that do not move

1. The map never mentions lights.
2. One clock — time comes from the audio, in seconds, from the start of the song.
3. The frame is a function of time alone.
4. Every map says who made it and how sure it is.

Everywhere else: choose the fastest thing and stop thinking about it.
