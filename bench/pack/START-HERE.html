Limelight · measurement lane · 2 September 2026 · sixteen days to KeyCode

1 · Why this is a lane and not a chore

In sixteen days a team of seven will make roughly two hundred changes to a system that turns music into light. Most of those changes will feel like improvements. Some fraction of them will be. Without an instrument, the difference between those two categories is invisible, and a project in that state does not fail loudly — it drifts, everyone stays busy, and the thing on stage on the eighteenth is whatever happened to survive.

There is also a structural reason this cannot sit with the person building the model. Someone who both builds a thing and decides what counts as correct will grade generously. Not through dishonesty — through hope.

2 · The two gates, and what neither can do

Two questions get asked about every version of this system, and they are not the same question. Conflating them is the most common way measurement work goes wrong.

3 · The instruments, in the order they pay off

1 · The tapping tool · highest leverage in the project right now

Truth files are the bottleneck for everything measured, and only Renjith can make them. Today that is an hour of careful listening per song, and he does not have six hours. Collapse it: play audio, tap a key on each beat, capture the timestamps, write the truth JSON.

Passes
Renjith produces a usable truth file for one song in under fifteen minutes, and a second listener's independent file agrees within the bench tolerance
Method
Test it against a click track of known tempo before letting a human near it. Recovered BPM must be within 0.2 of the true value
Trap
Timestamp against the audio clock — <audio>.currentTime or the audio callback time — never time.time(). Wall clock drifts against the audio at tens of milliseconds a minute, which is inside the tolerance you are trying to measure, so it corrupts every truth file systematically and invisibly. Also: only ask for a thirty-second window, per the protocol.

2 · Cross-check the bench against something published

bench/bench.py is homegrown. If its beat F-measure disagrees with mir_eval on the same two arrays, ours is wrong, and every decision made on it is wrong. Reconcile once, early, and write down why any remaining difference is intentional.

Passes
agreement to three decimal places on ten pairs, or a written reason
Trap
The conventions differ before the maths does. Check first whether the reference implementation trims beats near the start of the track and how it resolves ties in matching — a discrepancy there looks like a bug in the metric and is not one.

3 · The population runner and the run record

One pair at a time answers nothing. Run every truth-and-candidate pair, aggregate per song and per level, and store the run: what code, what map version, what tolerances, what date. Without a run record, "it got better" is unfalsifiable.

Given
cohort/ — twelve synthetic candidates at four graded tiers across three songs, plus stand-in truths. Filenames leak nothing
Passes
your aggregate recovers all five facts in cohort/EXPECTED.md without being told them. Read that file after you have built something
Trap
One candidate in the cohort is malformed on purpose. A harness that silently scores a malformed map produces a number that means nothing and will do so quietly forever. A refusal and a zero are different facts.

4 · The regression alarm

The characteristic way a two-week machine-learning sprint fails is that someone improves level 1 and silently breaks level 3, and nobody notices until the demo. Compare each run against the last and shout when any per-level number goes down.

Passes
replay two stored runs where you know a number dropped, and the alarm fires on exactly that number
Trap
Do not add a single headline score. It will hide exactly the metrics that are awkward to move — span shape and holds accuracy — and those are the two a room makes obvious.

5 · The taste gate

Render two versions of the same fifteen seconds, show them to a human, record which was chosen. That is how taste stops being an opinion and becomes data — and those recorded preferences are the beginning of the correction corpus, which is the part of this project with long-term value.

Passes
Renjith completes twenty comparisons in five minutes without touching a config file, and the log is unambiguous about what was compared
Trap
Blind it. If the labels are visible, you are measuring expectation. And keep the clip short — fifteen seconds, matched start point, or the comparison is between two different pieces of music rather than two versions of one.

6 · Confidence calibration · week two

Does the confidence number predict anything? Bucket predictions by claimed confidence and plot observed accuracy against it.

Passes
a reliability curve, and a one-sentence verdict on whether confidence is usable for ranking
Trap
This matters because the correction interface ranks by confidence, which makes a confidently wrong model worse than one that abstains.

  1. bench/selftest.py. Eleven deliberate breakages. Learn what each metric is blind to before you extend anything — a 30 ms jitter is invisible by design.
  2. Read example/expected-report.txt and find the four planted lessons.
  3. Score the twelve candidates in cohort/. Try to recover the tier ordering with an aggregate of your own design. Then, and only then, open cohort/EXPECTED.md.
  4. Sketch the tapping tool. It is small, it is the bottleneck for the whole project, and it can plausibly be working tomorrow.

The failure mode of this lane is drifting toward what is easy to measure. Beat accuracy is easy, so it gets polished. Span shape and holds are awkward, so they get quietly dropped — and they are the two that a room makes obvious. Keep the ugly numbers in the report.

One rule above all the others: never move a tolerance to make a number look better. You are the person best placed to do it and the only person who would not be caught. Every tolerance in bench/README.md has a stated reason — argue it in the open, before an experiment, never after seeing a result.
