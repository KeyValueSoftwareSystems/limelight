What the music is doing

Loudness answers "how loud", not "what is happening". This row answers the second question. The band is whether the loudness is rising, steady or falling. The filled curve is the build — how much this bar sounds like a riser, which is the sub being filtered out while noise climbs. ▲ is where the loudness slams up, ▼ where it drops away; the solid ones are structural, the hollow ones smaller steps.

A section edge drawn as a dashed amber line in the strip above is one the music arrives at gradually — the two sections really do differ, but nothing happens on that bar, so there is no instant to cue. Don't Look Down's second verse is one: the bass climbs from 0.09 to 0.98 across eight bars while the loudness stays flat.

How the sound itself changes

Press play and the page follows the music at a zoom where a beat is visible; press Fit to see the whole song again. Six things the computer measures every bar that the rows above do not show. The number on the right of each row is its value where you are now. Click anywhere to jump there.

An amber dashed line is where the song changes tempo — a real event you can hear, and one the rest of this page can only show by everything after it shifting.

Each row carries a number saying how much it actually tells you about this song: how much more the parts differ from each other than a part differs from its own two halves. Above about 1.3 the row is following the music; at 1.0 a part differs from itself as much as from any other part, and the row is noise you could light by mistake. No curve is reliable on every song — Pump says little on ten of the twenty-eight, Brightness on six.

The rhythm

Where the hits actually land inside one bar — the pattern, not how many. Each column is a sixteenth, left to right across a bar; a filled block is a hit there. This is the thing you hum back, and it is the only row on this page that says what the rhythm is rather than how much of it there is.

The tune

The computer followed the pitch of the singing and of the lead instrument and wrote down every note. Below, each mark is one note: further right is later in the song, higher up is a higher note, and the faint line joins notes that follow each other, so you can see the tune rise and fall. This is how it knows when a tune comes back later — which is what the lighting reader uses to repeat a look. Click a shaded stretch to hear a tune that repeats, or click anywhere to jump there.

The words

The computer listened to the singing and wrote down every word with the moment it was sung, then listened a second time in differently placed chunks and kept score of which words both passes produced. The number on the left is how much of that line both passes agreed on. Lines below 0.6 are marked — those are usually where the voice is buried under the production, and the computer is guessing. Click a line to hear it.
